# Portfolio research and storytelling principles

Lesley's direction: make data understandable to someone arriving without context, and pursue a useful, less-travelled question even when the dataset is familiar. Novelty is not required; a defensible perspective is.

## Before building a project
1. Review a bounded, varied sample of relevant work: original source, popular analyses, substantial repositories and applicable research. Record search terms, date, links, what was actually read and coverage gaps. Never describe a sample as all existing work.
2. Identify the common questions and conclusions. Choose a specific business decision that those examples leave unresolved or treat lightly.
3. Distinguish a less-common framing in the reviewed sample from a genuinely new finding. Do not claim first, unique or unprecedented without evidence.
4. Frame a question with plausible competing explanations. State what result would change the proposed action. Do not choose a provocative headline and force the data to support it.
5. Check whether the available records can answer the question. Separate descriptive evidence, proxies, assumptions, scenarios and causal effects.

## Throughout the story
- Explain the business situation before the metric.
- Ask what a familiar KPI hides, who an average overlooks, or what trade-off a decision creates.
- Compare like with like and retain relevant denominators, exclusions and uncertainty.
- Carry one central question through analysis, visuals, recommendations and acceptance criteria.
- Present outcomes that challenge the starting hypothesis as readily as those that support it.
- Translate evidence into a proposed decision and a way to test it; do not invent realised impact.

## Attribution and contribution
Credit the dataset and licence. Cite any borrowed method, framing, code or substantive insight beside its use. Distinguish independently computed results from ideas learned from others. Keep an honest AI-assistance statement. Similar findings from a shared dataset are not proof of copying, but originality must never be exaggerated.

## Practical test
Can a recruiter say what decision the project helps make, what makes its treatment thoughtful, what evidence supports it and what it cannot establish—without Lesley explaining it live?
